# 06 — BOSS: The Gimbal

> [!danger] Boss Fight
> This is the hardest part of the whole project. It's where most DIY sticks succeed or feel like cheap toys. Bring everything from Quests 3 and 5.

## 1. What Is a Gimbal?

> [!tip]- Retrieval Cue
> What is a gimbal? Where else are gimbals used (cameras, ships, rockets)? What's the common idea?

> [!tip]- Retrieval Cue
> A flight stick needs to tilt forward/back **and** left/right independently. How can two pivots be arranged so that each one only sees *its own* axis?

## 2. Study the Masters

> [!todo] Field Mission
> Find three open-source 3D-printable joystick gimbals (Printables, Thingiverse, GitHub). For each: sketch how the axes are arranged, how it re-centers, and where the sensor sits. What did each designer prioritise?

> [!tip]- Retrieval Cue
> What's the difference between a "cam-based" centering mechanism and springs pulling directly on the stick? Which feels better in commercial sticks, and why?

## 3. The Forces

> [!tip]- Retrieval Cue
> What does a bearing actually do? Why put one in a pivot instead of just a plastic hole?

> [!tip]- Retrieval Cue
> Where in your gimbal will the forces be highest? Which part would break first if you yanked the stick? How would you reinforce it?

> [!tip]- Retrieval Cue
> What makes centering feel "crisp" versus "mushy"? What would you change to adjust it?

## 4. Design Yours

> [!todo] Field Mission
> Sketch your own gimbal concept, on paper first, then as a rough SolidWorks assembly. Use mates to sweep it through its full range. Does anything collide?

> [!question]- Feynman Check
> Present your gimbal design to someone as if it were a FIRST Global design review. Justify every choice, and name its weakest point.

> [!success]- Unlocks
> **Design Decisions.md**: DD-04 Gimbal Architecture, and permission to print v1.
